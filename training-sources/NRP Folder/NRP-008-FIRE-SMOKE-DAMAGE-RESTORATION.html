1. Fire Damage Classifications

Protein Fire Residue

Source: Kitchen fires, burnt food, cooking oils

Characteristics: Nearly invisible, strong pungent odour, yellowish coating

Cleaning Method: Alkaline cleaners, enzyme treatments

PPE Required: N95 mask, chemical-resistant gloves

Synthetic/Plastic Fire Residue

Source: Plastics, rubber, synthetic materials

Characteristics: Black, smeary, difficult to clean, toxic

Cleaning Method: Solvent-based cleaners, dry cleaning sponges first

PPE Required: P100 respirator, chemical suit, eye protection

Natural/Organic Fire Residue

Source: Wood, paper, natural fibres

Characteristics: Grey/black, dry, powdery residue

Cleaning Method: HEPA vacuum first, wet cleaning safe

PPE Required: N95 mask, standard protective clothing

Fuel Oil Soot

Source: Furnace puff-backs, oil heater malfunctions

Characteristics: Sticky, smears easily, strong petroleum odour

Cleaning Method: Degreasing agents, may require professional

PPE Required: Full-face respirator, chemical-resistant suit

Australian Fire Services Requirement: All fire damage sites must be inspected and cleared by Fire & Rescue NSW (or state equivalent) before restoration begins. Structural engineer certification required for damage affecting load-bearing elements.
