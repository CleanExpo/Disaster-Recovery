📚 IICRC S500 Standards & Classifications

Industry-standard water damage classification system used globally

💧 Water Categories

  • Category 1: Clean water (broken pipes, appliances)
  • Category 2: Grey water (washing machines, dishwashers)
  • Category 3: Black water (sewage, flooding)

📊 Damage Classes

  • Class 1: Minimal absorption (concrete, tile)
  • Class 2: Significant absorption (carpet, cushions)
  • Class 3: Maximum absorption (insulation, hardwood)
  • Class 4: Specialty drying (stone, concrete)

🌡️ Environmental Factors

  • Temperature: Optimal 21-27°C for drying
  • Humidity: Target <40% RH for completion
  • Air Movement: 1 air mover per 10m² minimum
  • Dehumidification: 20L/day per 100m³ volume

⏱️ Drying Timeframes

  • Class 1: 2-3 days typical
  • Class 2: 3-5 days typical
  • Class 3: 5-7 days typical
  • Class 4: 7-14 days typical

📝 Professional Assessment - Water Damage Restoration

Instructions: Answer all questions based on IICRC S500 standards and Australian practices. Passing score: 80%

Question 1: According to IICRC S500, what is the target relative humidity for completion of structural drying?

Answer: b) Less than 40%. IICRC S500 establishes 40% RH as the target for structural drying completion, ensuring materials reach equilibrium moisture content and preventing secondary damage.

Question 2: What is the minimum air mover capacity required per 10m² for Class 2 damage?

Answer: c) 1000 CFM. For Class 2 damage, IICRC recommends minimum 1000 CFM per 10m² to ensure adequate air movement across wet surfaces and accelerate evaporation.

Question 3: In Australian conditions, what temperature range is optimal for structural drying?

Answer: b) 21-27°C. This temperature range optimizes evaporation rates while maintaining energy efficiency and occupant comfort in Australian climates.
