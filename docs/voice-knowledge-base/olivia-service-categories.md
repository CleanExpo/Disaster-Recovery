# Service Categories — What NRPG Contractors Are Approved For

_Last updated: 2026-04-26._

NRPG approves contractors for **one or more** of the following damage
categories. Competency is assessed per category. A contractor can
extend their approved categories at any time after onboarding by
clearing the relevant competency test.

## Water damage

The largest single category by volume. Includes:

- Burst pipe escape-of-water events.
- Roof leak water ingress.
- Hot-water-system failures.
- Storm-driven water entry (intersects with **storm**).
- Sewage backup (intersects with **sewage** — different IICRC standard).

Standard: **IICRC S500 — Water Damage Restoration**.

## Fire damage

Direct fire damage plus secondary smoke and soot. Includes:

- Structural fire damage (post-firefighting).
- Kitchen and electrical fires.
- Smoke damage to soft furnishings, ducts, and HVAC.
- Soot remediation across affected surfaces.

Standard: **IICRC S700 — Fire & Smoke Damage Restoration**.

## Flood damage

Distinct from "water damage" because of the contamination class. Flood
water is treated as Category 3 ("black water") under IICRC S500 —
materials in contact often must be discarded rather than restored.
Includes:

- Riverine and flash flooding.
- Storm surge.
- Sewer-backed flooding (overlaps with sewage).

Standards: IICRC S500 with Category 3 protocols + S520 if mould has
established.

## Storm damage

Combined wind, rain, hail, and lightning damage. Often overlaps with:

- Water (driven rain ingress through compromised roofing).
- Structural (impact damage from debris).
- Fire (lightning strikes).

Make-safe work is the priority — board-ups, tarping, electrical
isolation — before remediation begins.

## Mould damage

Always a downstream consequence of moisture, but treated as its own
category because remediation requires:

- Containment to prevent spore spread.
- HEPA filtration during work.
- Verification testing post-remediation.

Standard: **IICRC S520 — Mould Remediation**.

## Biohazard

Crime scenes, unattended deaths, infectious-disease decontamination,
and similar. Requires specialist PPE, disposal pathways under state
regulations, and trauma-informed handling.

Standard: **IICRC S540 — Trauma & Crime Scene**.

## Sewage

Sewage spills, backups, and overflow. Treated as Category 3 under
S500 — heavy decontamination protocols. Often overlaps with **water**
and **mould** categories on the same job.

## Vehicle impact

Vehicle-into-building damage. The restoration scope often includes
structural assessment, water and gas isolation, glass and façade
make-safe, and (often) insurance liaison for both the building owner
and the at-fault driver's insurer.

## Trauma scene cleanup

Distinct from biohazard in scope — trauma scenes are typically
residential and emotionally sensitive, requiring discretion plus the
S540 standard. Scope often includes liaison with police, coroner, and
family services.

## How matching works

When a claim comes in, NRPG matches based on:

- **Damage category** — the claim describes one or more categories.
- **Postcode / territory** — the closest contractor approved for that
  category in the relevant geography is preferred.
- **Availability** — current load and SLA targets.
- **Performance** — KPIs from prior jobs (response time, completion
  rate, client rating).

A contractor approved for more categories is matched into a wider
pool of jobs. That said, applying for **every** category is not the
goal — IICRC competency per category matters more than breadth.
