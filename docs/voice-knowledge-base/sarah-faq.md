# Sarah FAQ — Common Claim Questions

_Last updated: 2026-04-26._

Sarah uses these answers to ground responses. For anything not here
she should refuse and offer to escalate to a human operator.

## Costs and quotes

**How much will the restoration cost?**
Cost depends on damage type, severity, and scope. Sarah cannot
quote a firm price — once a contractor has assessed the property
in person they produce a costed Scope of Works. That's the firm
quote.

**Can I get a quote over the phone?**
No. We don't provide firm quotes without an on-site assessment.
The cost guides on `disasterrecovery.com.au/guides` give indicative
ranges sourced from industry data — those are starting points only.

## Insurance

**Will my insurance cover this?**
Coverage depends on your policy. Disaster Recovery isn't an
insurance broker or authorised representative — we can't make
coverage promises. The contractor we match you with will work to
your insurer's documentation standard, which helps with claim
approval, but acceptance is between you and your insurer.

**Do you bill my insurer directly?**
No. The contractor bills you directly for the restoration work.
You then claim that cost through your insurance per your policy.

**Will I get my excess back?**
That depends on your policy and your claim outcome — talk to your
insurer.

## Timing

**How fast can someone get to my property?**
Subject to the contractor's availability and your location. For
emergency damage we route to a contractor as fast as we can; for
non-urgent damage scheduling is at the contractor's pace.

**How long does restoration take?**
Depends on severity:

- A small water-damage job may be done in 3-5 days.
- A typical residential water claim with reconstruction: 2-4 weeks.
- A serious fire claim with reconstruction: 6-12 weeks or longer.

These are indicative — your contractor's Scope of Works will give
you a real timeline.

## The contractor

**Who is the contractor?**
An IICRC-certified business in the NRPG network. They're
independent — they're not employed by Disaster Recovery. Sarah
won't share contractor names or business details over the phone;
that's confirmed in writing once a contractor accepts your claim.

**Can I pick which contractor?**
The system matches based on category, postcode, availability, and
performance. We can't take preferences for specific contractors on
the lodgment call.

**Are they insured?**
Yes — every contractor in the network has current public liability
and workers compensation insurance, verified at onboarding and at
annual renewal.

## Process

**What happens after I lodge?**
Internal triage matches you with a contractor. The contractor
contacts you to arrange an on-site assessment. They produce a
Scope of Works. You agree the scope. They do the work. You sign
off when complete.

**Do I need to be home?**
For the initial assessment, yes. For some of the remediation work,
not always — your contractor will tell you.

**What's "make-safe"?**
The emergency stabilisation work that stops damage getting worse —
board-ups, tarps, water extraction, electrical isolation. It's the
first thing a contractor does on an active claim, before the main
remediation begins.

## Emergencies

**Active fire / collapse / injury / gas leak?**
Call **000** first. Once everyone is safe, call Disaster Recovery
or lodge online.

**Sewage backup or contamination?**
Don't try to clean it yourself — sewage carries pathogens. Ventilate
the area if safe to do so, keep people and pets away, and lodge a
claim. The contractor will handle it under IICRC Category 3
protocols.

## Privacy

**What happens to my information?**
Captured under the Australian Privacy Principles. Used to match you
with a contractor, support your claim, and meet record-keeping
obligations. See `disasterrecovery.com.au/privacy` for the full
notice. You can request access or deletion via the contact form.

## Out-of-scope

If a caller asks about anything Sarah can't answer (specific
contractor identities, claim outcomes, insurance approval, financial
advice, other people's claims, internal systems), Sarah should
respond with the canonical refusal line:

> "I'm only able to help with lodging your claim — I don't have
> visibility into that. Would you like to continue with your claim
> details, or would you prefer I put you through to a human
> operator?"
