# Lodging a Claim with Disaster Recovery — Process Overview

_Last updated: 2026-04-26._

This document describes the property-owner claim flow. Sarah uses it
to ground factual answers about what happens after a claim is lodged.

## Step 1 — Lodge the claim

Where: `disasterrecovery.com.au/claim` (web form) or by talking to
Sarah directly.

Information captured:

- Caller's name and contact details (phone, email).
- Property address — the location where the damage occurred.
- Damage type — water, fire, mould, storm, flood, biohazard, sewage,
  vehicle impact, or trauma.
- Brief description of what happened.
- Insurance details — insurer name and policy reference (optional;
  many claims proceed without these).
- Urgency — emergency / urgent / scheduled.

The claim is saved as soon as the form is submitted.

## Step 2 — Triage

Internal review confirms the damage type, severity, and response
window. The claim is matched against the contractor pool by:

- Damage category.
- Postcode / territory.
- Contractor availability.
- Contractor performance KPIs.

Triage is typically same-business-day for new claims and within the
hour for emergencies. Subject to assessment.

## Step 3 — Dispatch

A matching IICRC-certified contractor from the NRPG network is
notified. The contractor accepts (or declines, in which case the
claim is rerouted). The accepted contractor is given the client's
contact details and arranges initial contact directly.

## Step 4 — Make-safe

For active damage events (burst pipes, ongoing leaks, structural
hazards), the contractor's first job is make-safe — stabilising the
property to prevent further damage. Make-safe is **distinct from
remediation**: it's the emergency board-up, water extraction,
tarping, electrical isolation work that stops the situation getting
worse.

## Step 5 — Scope of works

The contractor produces a Scope of Works document. This is the
costed plan covering everything they'll do — extraction, drying,
mould prevention, reconstruction (if needed). The Scope of Works
is what the client (and their insurer, if relevant) reviews and
agrees before remediation begins.

## Step 6 — Remediation

The main restoration work — drying, dehumidification, mould
remediation, surface restoration, reconstruction. Timeline depends
on severity; a small water-damage job may be done in a few days, a
large fire damage job may take weeks.

## Step 7 — Sign-off

Once the contractor has completed the work, the client signs off.
This triggers final billing (contractor → client direct) and closes
the claim record on the NRPG side.

## Costs

Restoration costs vary widely by damage type and severity. Disaster
Recovery's role is to match you with a certified contractor — the
contractor provides the firm Scope of Works and quote. We don't
quote firm prices on the phone or via the AI.

## Insurance

If the damage is covered by your insurance:

- The contractor will work to your insurer's documentation standard
  (this is one reason we use IICRC-certified contractors — their
  documentation is widely accepted).
- The contractor can liaise directly with the loss adjuster on your
  behalf if you authorise it.
- Disaster Recovery is **not** an insurance broker, agent, or
  authorised representative. We don't provide insurance advice and
  we don't bill the insurer.
- Acceptance of the work and any claim outcome depends on your
  policy and your insurer — we cannot promise approval.

## Emergencies

If the situation is a life-safety emergency — active fire, structural
collapse, someone injured, someone trapped, gas leak, electrical
hazard with water — call **000** first, then Disaster Recovery once
everyone is safe.

Sarah will route emergency callers to a human operator immediately.
