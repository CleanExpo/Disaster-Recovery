# NRPG Contractor Onboarding — The Seven-Step Process

_Last updated: 2026-04-26._

Onboarding follows a staged process so applicants can save and resume
between steps. Specific timeframes vary case-by-case; do not promise
turnaround times — say "subject to assessment".

## Step 1 — Application started

Where: `disasterrecovery.com.au` → contractor section → **Apply**

What happens:

- The applicant creates a profile with their business contact details.
- They tell us about their company — trading name, ABN, principal
  contact, business address.
- They indicate which damage categories they want to be approved for
  (one or more of the nine; see service-categories doc).
- The application is staged — applicants can save progress and return.

## Step 2 — Documents submitted

What the applicant uploads:

- **Australian Business Number (ABN)** — for AU applicants.
  NZ applicants provide an equivalent business identifier.
- **IICRC registration evidence** — for each damage category being
  applied for. The applicant provides their IICRC registration number;
  NRPG verifies against the public IICRC register.
- **Public liability insurance** — current certificate of currency
  (COI). Minimum cover requirements depend on the categories applied
  for; the application surface lists the threshold.
- **Workers compensation insurance** — current COI where required by
  the applicant's state or territory.
- **Trade licences** — any state or territory licences relevant to
  the work (e.g. plumbing, electrical, asbestos).
- **Identity** — proof of identity for the principal of the business.

All documents are stored encrypted at rest. The applicant can re-upload
a renewed document at any time after approval.

## Step 3 — Background check

Two checks run in parallel:

- **Identity verification** — confirms the principal is who they say
  they are.
- **Professional verification** — confirms the IICRC registration is
  current and the insurance certificates are valid.

Background check outcomes are typically returned within a few business
days. Subject to assessment.

## Step 4 — Competency tests

Short assessments per damage category. The tests confirm the team
understands the relevant IICRC standards and can apply them in the
field. For water damage that means S500; for mould, S520; and so on.

The tests are not pass/fail in a punitive sense — they let NRPG match
applicants to categories they're competent in, rather than approve
them for every category indiscriminately.

## Step 5 — Agreement signed

A digital contractor agreement covers:

- The applicant's obligations as a network contractor.
- NRPG's obligations to the applicant.
- The dispatch process — how jobs are routed.
- The platform fee structure and subscription terms.
- Confidentiality, data handling, and dispute resolution.
- Termination and offboarding terms.

The agreement is signed electronically. The applicant gets a PDF copy.

## Step 6 — First subscription payment

A recurring subscription that gives the contractor access to the
network, the dispatch system, and ongoing standards support. The
specific subscription rate is **confirmed in writing during
onboarding** — Olivia will not quote a figure on a call.

Subscription payments are processed via Stripe. Contractors can
update payment methods in the contractor portal.

## Step 7 — Approved

Once all prior steps are cleared:

- The contractor's profile goes live.
- Their service territory is assigned based on their stated service
  area and existing network coverage in that geography.
- They appear in the dispatch pool for matching jobs in their
  territory and approved categories.

The contractor portal gives access to:

- Active job offers and their status.
- A history of completed jobs.
- KPI metrics (response time, completion rate, client rating).
- Document renewal reminders (insurance, IICRC).
- Subscription and platform-fee history.

## After onboarding

- **Annual renewal:** insurance certificates and IICRC registrations
  are checked annually. Renewal reminders are automatic.
- **Adding categories:** existing contractors can apply for additional
  damage categories at any time — they only need to clear the
  competency test for the new category.
- **Changing territory:** territory adjustments are reviewed quarterly
  to balance coverage across the network.
