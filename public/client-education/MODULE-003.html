MODULE-003: Choosing the Right Contractor

Duration: 10 minutes | Category: Contractor Selection

Introduction

Selecting the right disaster recovery contractor is critical for successful restoration and insurance claim approval. This module explains how to verify credentials and what makes NRPG contractors uniquely qualified.

Why IICRC Certification Matters

What is IICRC?

Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification - the global standard for disaster restoration professionals.

IICRC Certification Means:

  • Completed rigorous training in proper restoration techniques
  • Passed comprehensive examinations
  • Stays current with continuing education requirements
  • Follows industry standards (S500 for water, S520 for mold)
  • Uses proper equipment and methodology
  • Maintains professional liability insurance

NRPG Advantage:

All NRPG contractors are IICRC-certified and undergo additional vetting:

  • Background checks
  • Insurance verification ($20M+ public liability required)
  • Mandatory NRPG training (18+ modules)
  • Ongoing performance monitoring
  • Client satisfaction ratings (minimum 4.5★ average)

Credentials to Verify

Essential Checks Before Hiring Any Contractor:

1. ABN Verification

Check at abr.business.gov.au

  • Verify business is registered and active
  • Match business name to contractor's claims
  • Check ABN is not cancelled or suspended

2. Insurance Coverage

Minimum Requirements:

  • Public Liability Insurance: $10M+ (NRPG requires $20M)
  • Professional Indemnity Insurance
  • Workers Compensation (if employees)
  • Request Certificate of Currency (must be current)

3. IICRC Certification

  • Request certification number
  • Verify at iicrc.org (international database)
  • Check certification is current (not expired)
  • Ensure certification matches service type (water, fire, mold)

4. References and Reviews

  • Check Google Reviews, ProductReview.com.au
  • Look for patterns (not just star ratings)
  • Request references from similar jobs
  • Check with insurer (many have preferred contractor lists)

Red Flags to Watch For

⚠️ Warning Signs of Unreliable Contractors:

  • Requests full payment upfront - Legitimate contractors work on payment schedules
  • No written contract or vague scope - Always get detailed written agreements
  • Pressure tactics: "Must start today or price doubles"
  • Cannot provide insurance proof - Never hire uninsured contractors
  • Cash-only payments - Legitimate businesses accept bank transfers/credit cards
  • No physical address or ABN - Red flag for fly-by-night operators
  • Unwilling to work with your insurer - Professional contractors expect this
  • No IICRC or trade certifications - Lack of professional qualifications

Getting Multiple Quotes

How Many Quotes Should You Get?

  • Emergency situations: 1-2 quotes (speed is critical)
  • Non-urgent repairs: 2-3 quotes (compare thoroughly)
  • Large projects ($25k+): 3 quotes minimum

What to Compare:

  • Total cost (not just the lowest - quality matters)
  • Scope of work (ensure all quote same work)
  • Timeline (realistic vs overpromising)
  • Warranty period (longer is better)
  • Payment terms (schedule, not all upfront)
  • Insurance compatibility (will they work with your insurer?)
Beware the Lowest Quote: Significantly lower quotes may indicate cut corners, cheaper materials, or unlicensed workers. Focus on value, not just price.

Contract Essentials

Every Contract Must Include:

  • Contractor business name, ABN, contact details
  • Your name and property address
  • Detailed scope of work (what will be done)
  • Materials specifications (brands, quality levels)
  • Total cost breakdown (labor, materials, GST)
  • Payment schedule (deposit, progress payments, final)
  • Start and completion dates
  • Warranty period (minimum 12 months recommended)
  • Insurance claim reference number
  • Dispute resolution process

Payment Schedule (Safe Practice)

Recommended Payment Structure:

  • Deposit: 10-20% to secure booking (NRPG: excess only)
  • Progress Payments: Tied to milestones (30%, 30%, final 40%)
  • Final Payment: Only after you approve completed work
  • Retention: Consider holding 5-10% for 30 days after completion
NRPG's Payment Model: We use pre-authorization holds (no money taken upfront). Final charge only occurs after you approve the completed work. This protects you while ensuring contractors are paid fairly.

Questions to Ask Contractors

  1. Are you IICRC certified? What certifications do you hold?
  2. Can you provide proof of insurance (Certificate of Currency)?
  3. How many similar jobs have you completed?
  4. Will you work directly with my insurance company?
  5. What is your process for this type of damage?
  6. How long will the work take?
  7. What warranty do you provide?
  8. Who will be doing the actual work? (employees or subcontractors)
  9. What equipment will you use?
  10. How will you protect unaffected areas during restoration?

Key Takeaways

  1. IICRC certification is essential - Industry standard for quality
  2. Verify ABN and insurance - Protect yourself legally
  3. Read reviews and check references - Past performance indicates future results
  4. Get written contracts - Verbal agreements are difficult to enforce
  5. Never pay full amount upfront - Protect your investment
  6. Watch for red flags - Pressure tactics, no insurance, cash-only
  7. NRPG contractors are pre-vetted - Save time and reduce risk
