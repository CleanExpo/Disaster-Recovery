MODULE-001: What to Do After Water Damage

Duration: 10 minutes | Category: Emergency Response

Introduction

Water damage is one of the most common and potentially devastating disasters affecting Australian properties. Whether from burst pipes, flooding, storm damage, or appliance failures, knowing how to respond quickly can minimize damage and reduce restoration costs.

⚠️ SAFETY FIRST

Do NOT enter water-damaged areas if:

  • Water is above ankle height
  • Electrical outlets are submerged or sparking
  • You smell gas
  • The ceiling is sagging or bulging
  • You see structural damage to walls or floors

In these cases, evacuate immediately and call emergency services (000).

Immediate Actions (First Hour)

1. Stop the Water Source (If Safe)

  • Burst pipe: Turn off main water valve
  • Leaking appliance: Unplug and turn off water supply
  • Roof leak: Place buckets to catch water, cover with tarp if accessible
  • Flooding: Use sandbags if time permits, otherwise evacuate
Note: If you cannot safely stop the water source, evacuate and call a plumber immediately. Do not risk your safety.

2. Turn Off Electricity (If Safe)

  • Turn off power at the main switchboard if water is near electrical outlets
  • Never touch electrical switches with wet hands
  • If you cannot safely access the switchboard, call an electrician
  • Do not use electrical appliances in wet areas

3. Document the Damage IMMEDIATELY

Essential Documentation Checklist:

  • Take photos of ALL affected areas (wide shots and close-ups)
  • Record video walkthrough with narration describing the damage
  • Note the time water damage was discovered
  • Photograph water source if identified
  • Document affected furniture, electronics, and belongings
  • Take photos of water level marks on walls
  • Save all photos with timestamps (most phones do this automatically)

4. Contact Your Insurance Company

  • Within 24 hours - Most policies require prompt notification
  • Have your policy number ready
  • Provide date, time, and cause of water damage
  • Ask about emergency accommodation if your home is uninhabitable
  • Request claim reference number and assessor contact details
  • Ask what immediate steps you're authorized to take
Important: Some policies require approval before hiring contractors. Always check with your insurer first to avoid claim denial.

5. Call Emergency Restoration Services

  • Contact NRPG or another IICRC-certified contractor within 24-48 hours
  • Water damage worsens quickly - mold can start growing within 24-48 hours
  • Professional extraction and drying prevents secondary damage
  • NRPG contractors are available 24/7 for emergencies

What NOT to Do

❌ Common Mistakes to Avoid:

  • Don't use household vacuum cleaners to remove water (electrical hazard)
  • Don't use standard fans without professional guidance (can spread mold spores)
  • Don't remove carpet or flooring yourself (may affect insurance claim)
  • Don't delay - every hour counts in preventing mold and structural damage
  • Don't throw away damaged items until your insurer has assessed them
  • Don't hire unlicensed contractors - may void your insurance

First 24-48 Hours

What You Can Do While Waiting for Contractors:

  • Remove standing water with mop and bucket (if safe)
  • Move furniture and belongings to dry areas
  • Open windows for ventilation (if weather permits)
  • Remove wet rugs, cushions, and soft furnishings
  • Prop up wet furniture cushions for air circulation
  • Place aluminum foil under furniture legs to prevent staining

What Professional Contractors Will Do:

  • Water extraction using industrial equipment
  • Moisture detection and mapping (thermal cameras, meters)
  • Install commercial dehumidifiers and air movers
  • Set up containment barriers to prevent spread
  • Monitor drying progress with daily readings
  • Apply antimicrobial treatments to prevent mold

Insurance Claim Checklist

Documents to Gather for Your Claim:

  • Insurance policy documents
  • Photos and videos of damage
  • Plumber's report (if burst pipe)
  • Contractor estimates and invoices
  • Receipts for emergency expenses (accommodation, meals)
  • Inventory of damaged items with approximate values
  • Purchase receipts for valuable items (if available)
  • Communication logs with insurer (dates, times, who you spoke with)

Cost Considerations

Typical Water Damage Restoration Costs (Australia):

  • Minor leak (single room): $1,500 - $3,000
  • Moderate damage (multiple rooms): $3,000 - $8,000
  • Severe flooding (whole property): $10,000 - $50,000+
  • Mold remediation (if delayed): Additional $2,000 - $10,000
Cost-Saving Tip: Quick action in the first 24 hours can reduce total costs by up to 50%. Delayed response leads to mold, structural damage, and much higher expenses.

Prevention Tips for the Future

  • Know the location of your main water shut-off valve
  • Inspect washing machine and dishwasher hoses annually
  • Clear gutters and downpipes regularly
  • Check roof annually (especially before storm season)
  • Install water leak detectors near appliances
  • Maintain plumbing (replace old pipes, fix leaks promptly)
  • Know who to call in emergencies (NRPG: 1800 NRPG)

Key Takeaways

  1. Safety first - Never risk electrocution or injury
  2. Act quickly - First 24-48 hours are critical
  3. Document everything - Photos, videos, receipts
  4. Contact insurer within 24 hours - Required by most policies
  5. Hire IICRC-certified contractors - Ensures insurance compliance
  6. Don't delay - Mold grows fast in Australian climate
